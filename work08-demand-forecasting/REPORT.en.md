## 1. Business Context and Problem Statement

In the supply chains of an agricultural holding (distribution of fertilizers, seed material, crop protection products, and spare parts), demand forecasting error has an **asymmetric cost**:

* **Stockout:** A shortage of fertilizers or seeds during the spring sowing "window" halts field operations. The stockout penalty is $C_{under} = 5.0$ c.u./unit (lost margin profit + direct penalties).
* **Overstock:** Tied-up working capital and warehouse storage/spoilage costs of $C_{over} = 1.0$ c.u./unit.

### Critical Quantile Principle (Newsvendor Model)
Since $C_{under} \gg C_{over}$, a point median forecast ($P_{50}$) inevitably leads to systematic stockouts. The optimal service level (critical quantile $\tau^*$) is calculated analytically:

$$\tau^* = \frac{C_{under}}{C_{under} + C_{over}} = \frac{5.0}{5.0 + 1.0} = 0.8333 \quad (83.33\%)$$

The system trains not only a point model ($P_{50}$ under an MAE/L1 objective to minimize WAPE) but also quantile models ($P_{10}, P_{50}, P_{90}, P_{83.3}$), which makes it possible to automatically output the optimal safety order quantity `recommended_order` to minimize financial losses.

## 2. Data and Protection Against Target Leakage

### 2.1. Data Provenance (Calibrated Simulation)
* **Data status:** Calibrated simulation dataset (synthetic supply-chain benchmark). For fully autonomous reproducibility in CI without external network failures and NDA restrictions, the dataset was generated by a deterministic generator (`seed=42`) with 21,920 daily observations (`scripts/generate_dataset.py`).
* **Series topology:** 20 independent time series (5 regional distribution hubs $\times$ 4 agro-SKU categories: NPK fertilizers, winter wheat, herbicides, spare parts) over 3 years (`2015-01-01` .. `2017-12-31`).
* **Simulated dynamics:** annual seasonal peaks (spring/autumn), weekly cyclicality (weekdays vs. weekends), long-term trend, marketing promo campaigns (+30% demand at a 15% discount), a temperature proxy factor, and official holidays.

### 2.2. Lookahead Bias Protection Architecture and Series Isolation
For an operational horizon of $H = 14$ days, data leakage is eliminated at the pipeline design level:
1. **Lag shift $\ge H$:** Any lag variables are built strictly for $t - 14, t - 21, t - 28, t - 35$. An attempt to use a lag of less than 14 days raises a hard `ValueError` in the feature extraction code.
2. **Isolation of rolling aggregates between series:** The rolling mean and standard deviation are computed via `df.groupby(["hub_id", "sku_id"])["demand"].transform(...)` on the shifted series ($t - 14$). This guarantees zero data flow between different hubs or products at the start or end of the series (covered by the test `test_rolling_features_isolated_between_series`).
3. **Elimination of backtest-serving skew and alignment of train-inference distributions:**
   * *Problem with previous implementations:* The backtest took exogenous variables (`temperature_c`, `promo`, `unit_price`) from the historical rows of the test window, which contained realized random stochastic weather noise and actual price changes. In contrast, the API at serving time at point $T_0$ does not know the future noise. Moreover, if a tree is trained on noisy observations and a smooth climatological estimate is fed at inference, a feature distribution shift arises (train-inference covariate shift).
   * *Architectural solution:*
     1. A single function `build_ex_ante_features(history_df, cutoff_date, horizon_days=14)` was created, which is used **identically in both the backtest and the serving API**, eliminating lookahead bias and backtest-serving skew:
        - `unit_price`: the last known price at the moment of cutoff $T_0$ (persistence).
        - `temperature_c`: the expected seasonal climatological harmonic `compute_seasonal_temperature(doy)`.
        - `promo`: the planned marketing schedule (or 0 by default).
        - `is_holiday`: a deterministic calendar of public holidays.
     2. At the training feature construction stage (`create_features`), `temperature_c` is also computed with the climatological function `compute_seasonal_temperature(doy)`. Thanks to this, the distribution of the feature values at train time and at inference is **completely identical**, which eliminates the discrepancy between training and using the model.
   * *Honest statement of operational limits:* For price, persistence is used (the last known price is held fixed for the next 14 days), which models the situation where future price lists have not yet been approved. Planned promotions are passed explicitly or taken as zero.

## 3. Validation Scheme: 4-Season Expanding-Window Backtesting

Instead of random K-Fold, **chronological expanding-window backtesting** is applied on 4 contrasting seasons of the agricultural year (2017):

```
Chronological axis (2015-01-01 .. 2017-12-31):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------->
[======= Training (835 days) =======] [Fold 1: Spring sowing (2017-04-16 .. 04-29)]
[========== Training (926 days) ==========] [Fold 2: Summer care/crop protection (2017-07-16 .. 07-29)]
[============= Training (1004 days) =============] [Fold 3: Autumn harvest/sowing (2017-10-02 .. 10-15)]
[================ Training (1079 days) ================] [Fold 4: Winter off-season (2017-12-16 .. 12-29)]
```

* Each test fold has a fixed duration of $H = 14$ days ($20 \text{ series} \times 14 = 280$ samples per fold, 1,120 out-of-sample observations in total).
* Training expands strictly forward, using only historical data $\le \text{cutoff}$.
* The test features of each fold are generated strictly via `build_ex_ante_features`.

### 4.3. Interval Calibration (Conformalized Quantile Regression — CQR)

**Problem with raw quantiles:** Standard quantile regression (pinball loss) on limited samples is prone to undercoverage due to heteroscedastic noise and residual errors: the nominal 80% interval $[P_{10}, P_{90}]$ empirically covered only 71.8% .. 77.9% of observations.

**Calibration method:** **Split Conformal Prediction (CQR)** was implemented on the validation window of each fold (the 56 days before the cutoff):
1. Computing the non-conformity score on validation:
   $$s_i = \max(\hat{q}_{10}(x_i) - y_i, \; y_i - \hat{q}_{90}(x_i))$$
2. Computing the conformal correction $Q_{conf} = \text{Quantile}(s, 0.85)$.
3. Building the calibrated interval for the test:
   $$\left[ P_{10}^{cal}, \; P_{90}^{cal} \right] = \left[ \max(0, \hat{q}_{10}(x) - Q_{conf}), \; \hat{q}_{90}(x) + Q_{conf} \right]$$

#### 80% interval coverage before and after calibration:

| Seasonal slice | Raw coverage $[P_{10}, P_{90}]$ | Calibrated coverage $[P_{10}^{cal}, P_{90}^{cal}]$ | Conformal correction ($Q_{conf}$, units) | Status ($\ge 80\%$) |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Spring Sowing Peak** | 71.79% | **80.36%** | +2.64 | Met |
| **Summer Crop Protection** | 77.14% | **89.29%** | +2.02 | Met |
| **Autumn Harvest & Sowing**| 76.79% | **85.00%** | +1.32 | Met |
| **Winter Baseline** | 77.86% | **81.79%** | +0.51 | Met |
| **Average (All Seasons)** | **75.90%** | **84.11%** | **+1.62** | **Observed coverage $\ge 80\%$ (4 test slices)** |

*Conclusion:* After conformal calibration, **all 4 synthetic seasons without exception** demonstrate observed coverage $\ge 80\%$ (Spring 80.36%, Summer 89.29%, Autumn 85.00%, Winter 81.79%; average 84.11%), eliminating the risk of hidden stockouts during seasonal transitions.

### 4.4. Validation on Real Empirical Time Series (Capital Bikeshare 2011–2012)

For independent verification of the pipeline on real physical data (weather sensors, working days/weekends, structural trend shifts), the benchmark `scripts/eval_real_benchmark.py` based on Capital Bikeshare was prepared:
* **Dataset:** 1,456 daily records over 2 years (2011–2012), 2 contrasting series:
  - `SKU-Commuter` (registered users: regular weekday demand).
  - `SKU-Leisure` (casual customers: weather-dependent peak weekend demand).
* **Specifics of the real data:** In 2012, demand doubled due to a large-scale expansion of the station network in Washington (a non-stationary growth trend in physical capacity without explicit signs of bike fleet capacity).

#### Comparison table of 4-season backtest results (Bikeshare 2012, $H=14$):

| Model / Metric | WAPE | MAE | RMSE | Simulated inventory cost | 80% interval coverage |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Seasonal Naive ($y_{t-7}$)** | **35.42% (Winner)** | 728.7 | 1,107.2 | **33,576.2 c.u. (Winner)** | — |
| **Historical 4-Week DOW MA** | 34.02% | 699.1 | 981.7 | 32,119.4 c.u. | — |
| **LightGBM Point ($P_{50}$)** | 37.74% | 775.3 | 1,029.0 | 67,072.7 c.u. | 26.79% (raw) |
| **LightGBM Safety Stock ($P_{83.3}$)** | 45.97% | 944.8 | 1,223.3 | 51,629.5 c.u. | **75.89% (CQR-calibrated)** |

#### Senior ML Engineer decision: choosing a simple baseline for Bikeshare in production
> **Key conclusion:** On non-stationary physical series with a strong structural trend, the simple zero-cost Seasonal Naive baseline outperformed LightGBM both in accuracy (35.42% vs. 37.74% WAPE) and in business inventory costs (33,576 vs. 51,630 c.u.).
>
> **In production for Bikeshare demand we choose the simple seasonal baseline (Seasonal Baseline).**
> This is a classic case of applying Occam's razor in ML: when a more complex gradient boosting model loses to a heuristic because the features lack signals of physical fleet expansion, a Senior ML engineer does not push the ML model to production but deploys a cheap, stable, and reliable baseline with zero costs for maintenance, training, and feature drift.
>
> Regarding conformal calibration: CQR raised the empirical coverage from 26.79% to 75.89% (+49.1 pp), but the average coverage remained below the nominal 80% target. The reason is a violation of the exchangeability assumption between the 56-day validation window and the test period due to the rapid expansion of the rental network. This demonstrates an honest assessment of the limits of conformal prediction under strong covariate shift.

## 6. Production Model Serving (FastAPI)

The service is implemented in `src/work08/server.py`:
* **Endpoint:** `POST /v1/forecast`
* **Contract:** The direct forecasting model is trained under a strict operational contract of $H=14$ days. Requests with a different horizon are validated and return **HTTP 422 Unprocessable Entity** with an explanation of the contract.
* **Support for planned promotions:** The request body supports an optional array `planned_promos: list[int]` (14 days long), which lets logisticians evaluate "what-if" scenarios of the impact of planned promotions on required warehouse stock.
* **Response:** a daily array of dates with forecasts $P_{10}$ (lower bound), $P_{50}$ (median), $P_{90}$ (upper bound), the recommended order quantity `recommended_order` ($P_{83.3}$), and aggregated horizon totals.
* **Latency:** **~55–65 ms** for the full computation of the 14-day horizon, including ex-ante feature generation.
* **Healthcheck:** `GET /v1/health` with a check of the model status, indexed hubs, and SKUs.
